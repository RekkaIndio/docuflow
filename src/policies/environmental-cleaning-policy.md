# Environmental Cleaning Policy

**Policy Title:** Environmental Cleaning Policy

**Effective Date:** [Insert Date]

**Review Date:** [Insert Date – usually annually]

**Version:** 1.0

**Applies to:** All staff, agency workers, contractors, and visitors at [INSERT CARE HOME NAME]


## 1. Policy Statement

At [INSERT CARE HOME NAME], we are committed to maintaining a clean, safe, and hygienic environment to protect residents, staff, and visitors from infection risks and to promote dignity and wellbeing.
Effective cleaning and hygiene practices are a vital part of infection prevention and control (IPC) and support compliance with statutory and regulatory requirements.

We aim to:

- Ensure high standards of cleanliness across all areas of the home
- Minimise the risk of cross-infection through effective cleaning procedures
- Provide a safe, welcoming, and comfortable environment for residents
- Comply with health and safety and infection control legislation


## 2. Purpose

This policy sets out:

- Standards and procedures for environmental cleaning
- Roles and responsibilities of staff in maintaining hygiene
- Guidance on cleaning methods, schedules, and record keeping
- Measures to manage infection risks, including during outbreaks


## 3. Scope

This policy applies to:

- All areas of the care home including bedrooms, communal spaces, bathrooms, kitchens, offices, and external areas
- All staff with cleaning responsibilities, including care, domestic, catering, and maintenance teams
- Contractors providing cleaning or waste management services


## 4. Roles and Responsibilities

**Registered Manager:**

- Ensures environmental cleaning policies and procedures are in place
- Provides resources, equipment, and training for staff
- Monitors compliance through audits and inspections

**Domestic / Cleaning Staff:**

- Carry out cleaning tasks in line with agreed schedules and procedures
- Use appropriate cleaning products, PPE, and equipment safely
- Complete cleaning records and report any issues immediately

**All Staff:**

- Maintain good standards of cleanliness in their work areas
- Support infection prevention by following cleaning protocols (e.g., spills, shared equipment)
- Report hazards or unclean areas promptly


## 5. Key Cleaning Standards and Procedures

### 5.1 General Cleaning

- Daily cleaning of resident rooms, bathrooms, and communal areas
- Regular cleaning of high-touch surfaces (door handles, handrails, switches)
- Use of colour-coded equipment to prevent cross-contamination


### 5.2 Bathrooms and Toilets

- Cleaned and disinfected at least daily and after heavy use
- Dedicated cleaning equipment must be used for these areas only


### 5.3 Kitchens and Food Preparation Areas

- Cleaned and sanitised before and after food preparation
- Food hygiene standards must be strictly followed in line with HACCP principles
- Fridges, freezers, and storage areas cleaned regularly and recorded


### 5.4 Resident Bedrooms

- Cleaned daily, with bed linen changed in line with schedules or when soiled
- Personal items handled respectfully and with consent
- Deep cleaning carried out on resident discharge or after infection


### 5.5 Spillages and Body Fluids

- Must be cleaned immediately using appropriate disinfectants
- Staff must use PPE and dispose of waste safely
- Incident must be recorded


### 5.6 Waste Disposal

- Waste segregated into domestic and clinical waste streams
- Sharps disposed of in approved sharps containers
- Waste bins emptied daily and cleaned regularly


### 5.7 Outbreak Situations

- Enhanced cleaning protocols implemented immediately
- Increased frequency of cleaning for high-touch areas
- Use of approved disinfectants in line with Public Health guidance


## 6. Equipment and Supplies

- Cleaning products must be approved for use in healthcare environments
- PPE (gloves, aprons, masks where required) must be worn during cleaning
- Cleaning equipment must be maintained, replaced when worn, and stored safely


## 7. Training and Education

- Staff involved in cleaning will receive training in:
  - Infection prevention and control (IPC)
  - Safe use of cleaning chemicals (COSHH)
  - Colour-coding systems and cross-contamination prevention
- Training records will be maintained and refreshed annually


## 8. Monitoring and Audit

- Cleaning schedules and records must be completed daily
- Regular audits and inspections carried out by managers
- Resident and family feedback considered as part of quality assurance
- Non-compliance or poor standards will result in corrective action
