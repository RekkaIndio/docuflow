# Weather-Related Risk (Heatwave & Cold Weather) Policy

## Policy Statement
At [INSERT CARE HOME NAME], we recognise that extreme weather conditions such as heatwaves or severe cold can significantly affect the health and wellbeing of vulnerable residents. We are committed to protecting residents and staff by preparing for, responding to, and managing weather-related risks in line with national guidance.
We aim to:

- Reduce risks to residents’ health from extreme temperatures
- Ensure staff are trained to recognise early signs of heat- or cold-related illness
- Maintain safe indoor environments during adverse weather
- Promote a proactive, preventative approach to seasonal risks

## Purpose
The purpose of this policy is to:

- Provide guidance on protecting residents during periods of extreme heat or cold
- Outline practical measures to prevent illness, dehydration, hypothermia, and other weather-related risks
- Define staff responsibilities for monitoring and responding to residents’ needs during adverse weather

## Scope
This policy applies to:

- All residents of [INSERT CARE HOME NAME]
- All staff and volunteers responsible for resident care and support
- The care home environment, including resident rooms, communal spaces, and outdoor areas

## Roles and Responsibilities

### Registered Manager:

- Oversees implementation of the policy and ensures staff are prepared for seasonal risks
- Liaises with local health authorities when alerts (e.g., Heat-Health Alerts, Cold Weather Alerts) are issued
- Ensures contingency plans and equipment are in place (e.g., fans, blankets, heating systems)

### All Staff:

- Monitor residents for signs of weather-related health risks (e.g., dehydration, heatstroke, hypothermia)
- Follow preventative measures set out in this policy
- Encourage residents to stay safe and hydrated during extreme weather
- Report any concerns or equipment failures immediately

### Maintenance Staff:

- Ensure heating and cooling systems are serviced and operational
- Check insulation, ventilation, and window safety measures regularly

## Preventative Measures

### 5.1 During Heatwaves

- Keep residents hydrated by offering cold drinks regularly
- Provide lighter meals and encourage consumption of fruit and vegetables
- Close curtains/blinds in sun-exposed rooms
- Use fans or air conditioning units safely where available
- Encourage residents to wear lightweight, loose-fitting clothing
- Avoid unnecessary outdoor activities during the hottest parts of the day
- Monitor residents closely for symptoms of heat exhaustion or dehydration

### 5.2 During Cold Weather

- Ensure heating systems are working and maintain indoor temperatures of at least 18°C (as per NHS guidance)
- Provide residents with appropriate clothing, blankets, and hot drinks
- Encourage light exercise or movement to maintain circulation
- Ensure windows and doors are draught-proofed while maintaining ventilation
- Pay extra attention to vulnerable residents at risk of hypothermia (e.g., frail, immobile, or those with chronic health conditions)
- Monitor for signs of cold-related illness such as shivering, confusion, or drowsiness

## Resident Care and Monitoring

- Risk assessments will identify residents particularly vulnerable to extreme weather (e.g., those with respiratory or cardiac conditions).
- Staff must complete additional monitoring during weather alerts, including hydration checks, temperature monitoring, and wellbeing observations.
- Records of interventions will be maintained in residents’ care plans.

## Communication

- National Heat-Health or Cold Weather Alerts from the UKHSA will be monitored and cascaded to staff.
- Residents and families will be informed of any precautionary measures in place.
- Visitors may be advised on suitable visiting times and safety precautions.

## Training and Awareness

- Staff will receive training on recognising and responding to weather-related risks.
- Induction will include guidance on the Heatwave Plan for England and Cold Weather Plan for England.

## Monitoring and Review

- Equipment (heating, cooling, ventilation) will be checked regularly and tested prior to seasonal risks.
- Policy compliance will be reviewed annually or after a weather-related incident.
- Lessons learned from heatwave/cold weather events will inform improvements.
