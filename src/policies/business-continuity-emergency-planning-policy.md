1. Policy Statement
At [INSERT CARE HOME NAME], we are committed to safeguarding the health, safety, and wellbeing of residents, staff, and visitors during emergencies and ensuring continuity of essential care services.
We aim to:
	•	Minimise risks and disruption caused by emergencies.
	•	Maintain safe and effective care delivery during incidents.
	•	Comply with the Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, CQC Fundamental Standards, and local authority requirements.
	•	Foster a culture of preparedness, resilience, and rapid recovery.

2. Purpose
This policy sets out the framework for:
	•	Identifying potential risks and emergencies.
	•	Maintaining business continuity during disruption.
	•	Responding effectively to incidents.
	•	Protecting residents, staff, and visitors during emergencies.
	•	Ensuring clear communication, roles, and responsibilities.

3. Scope
This policy applies to:
	•	All staff, agency workers, and volunteers.
	•	Residents and visitors during an emergency.
	•	All operational areas of [INSERT CARE HOME NAME], including care delivery, administration, and facilities.

4. Roles and Responsibilities
Registered Manager (Incident Lead):
	•	Has overall responsibility for business continuity and emergency planning.
	•	Maintains and reviews the Business Continuity Plan (BCP).
	•	Liaises with local authorities, emergency services, and commissioners.
	•	Ensures staff receive training in emergency procedures.
Senior Staff / Deputies:
	•	Lead the response if the Registered Manager is unavailable.
	•	Coordinate staff, residents, and resources during emergencies.
	•	Ensure incident records and reports are completed.
All Staff:
	•	Familiarise themselves with emergency plans and procedures.
	•	Follow instructions during incidents.
	•	Report risks, hazards, or potential threats immediately.

5. Risk Identification and Planning
Potential risks include (but are not limited to):
	•	Fire and Evacuation
	•	Severe Weather (floods, snow, storms, heatwaves)
	•	Loss of Utilities (electricity, gas, water, heating)
	•	Infectious Disease Outbreaks (e.g., flu, COVID-19, norovirus)
	•	Staff Shortages
	•	IT or Communications Failure
	•	Security Threats / Intruders
Risk assessments will be carried out regularly, and contingency plans developed for each risk.

6. Business Continuity Measures
	•	Critical Services Identified – e.g., resident care, medication management, safeguarding.
	•	Alternative Arrangements – backup suppliers, temporary staffing, manual record keeping.
	•	Emergency Contacts – up-to-date list of key contacts (staff, suppliers, emergency services).
	•	Relocation Plan – arrangements with local services or other care providers if evacuation or relocation is necessary.

7. Emergency Response Procedures
In the event of an emergency:
	•	Immediate Action – ensure resident and staff safety.
	•	Raise Alarm / Notify – contact emergency services if required.
	•	Activate Plan – follow the relevant emergency protocol.
	•	Communication – keep staff, residents, families, and authorities informed.
	•	Maintain Care – prioritise essential care tasks for residents.
	•	Record Keeping – document all actions, decisions, and communications.

8. Training and Testing
	•	All staff receive training on emergency procedures at induction and through refresher sessions.
	•	Fire drills and other emergency exercises conducted at least twice per year.
	•	Business continuity plans tested periodically through desktop or live exercises.
	•	Lessons learned incorporated into revised plans.

9. Recovery and Review
	•	Post-incident reviews carried out to identify lessons learned.
	•	Recovery plans implemented to restore full service as quickly as possible.
	•	Reports shared with relevant authorities (CQC, commissioners, local authority) if required.
	•	Plans updated following incidents, audits, or annual reviews.
