1. Policy Statement
At [INSERT CARE HOME NAME], we respect the right of every resident to make informed decisions about their care and treatment. We will always seek valid consent before providing any care, support, or medical treatment.
This policy ensures our practices are compliant with:
	•	Mental Capacity Act 2005
	•	Care Act 2014
	•	Human Rights Act 1998
	•	CQC Fundamental Standards – Regulation 11 (Need for Consent)

2. Purpose
The purpose of this policy is to:
	•	Ensure consent is obtained before any care or treatment
	•	Support residents to make informed choices
	•	Provide guidance for staff when a person lacks capacity
	•	Promote autonomy, dignity, and human rights

3. Definitions
Consent:
The voluntary and informed agreement to a proposed intervention by a person with capacity to make that decision.
Mental Capacity:
The ability to make a specific decision at the time it needs to be made. Assessed in line with the Mental Capacity Act 2005.
Best Interests:
If a person lacks capacity, decisions must be made in their best interests, using the least restrictive option.

4. Principles of Consent
	•	Consent must be voluntary, informed, and given by someone with capacity.
	•	Consent can be:
	•	Verbal: spoken agreement
	•	Written: signed consent forms
	•	Implied: through actions (e.g., offering an arm for blood pressure check)
	•	Residents can withdraw consent at any time.
	•	All staff must respect a person’s right to refuse care, even if this results in risk (unless safeguarding concerns apply).

5. Assessing Capacity
Staff must assume capacity unless there is reason to believe otherwise.
A capacity assessment must consider:
	•	Can the person understand the information relevant to the decision?
	•	Can they retain that information long enough to make a decision?
	•	Can they use or weigh that information?
	•	Can they communicate their decision (by any means)?
If a resident lacks capacity, a best interest decision must be made in line with the Mental Capacity Act 2005.

6. Best Interests Decision-Making
When making decisions for someone who lacks capacity, staff must:
	•	Involve the resident as far as possible
	•	Consider their past and present wishes, beliefs, and values
	•	Consult with family, advocates, and professionals
	•	Choose the least restrictive option
Where appropriate, an Independent Mental Capacity Advocate (IMCA) must be involved.

7. Consent in Specific Situations
Personal Care:
	•	Consent must be gained before delivering any care.
	•	Residents must be offered choices and supported to express preferences.
Medication:
	•	Residents must be informed about what the medication is for and side effects.
	•	If a resident refuses medication, this must be respected and documented.
Healthcare / Treatment:
	•	Written consent is required for procedures such as vaccinations or dental treatment.
	•	If capacity is lacking, a best interest decision will be made in collaboration with healthcare professionals.
Photographs / Recordings:
	•	Explicit written consent is required before taking or using any images or recordings of residents.

8. Recording Consent
	•	All consent (verbal, written or implied) must be documented in the resident’s care records.
	•	If consent is refused, this must be recorded along with actions taken and alternatives considered.
	•	Capacity assessments and best interest decisions must be clearly documented.

9. Staff Responsibilities
	•	All staff must receive training on consent and mental capacity.
	•	Staff must know how to assess capacity and follow the Mental Capacity Act Code of Practice.
	•	Staff must not provide care or treatment without appropriate consent or legal authority.
